      📖 Plain-English Guide

      How the Ranking System Works in Plain English

      An intuitive explanation of the scoring mechanisms, track weights, and rating calculations.
      ⚡ The Executive Summary (TL;DR)
      Your global rating combines two components: 70% Peak Quality (your top 6 tracks) and 30% Versatility (your top 15 tracks). World Records and high placements on competitive tracks earn higher point values. Completing additional tracks beyond your top 15 gives zero extra points, ensuring that rankings reward placement quality rather than playtime.

      1. Why Simple Leaderboard Systems Fail in Racing

      Common ranking systems encounter specific failure modes in time-attack racing:

      ❌ The "Total Points" Trap (Playtime Grinding)
      If a system simply sums points across all 78 tracks, someone who plays 300 hours and finishes 25th on 70 small community tracks will easily beat a World Champion who holds 8 World Records on the hardest official campaign tracks. That rewards who has the most free time, not who drives the fastest.
      ❌ The "Elo / Matchmaking" Trap (Exploration Penalties)
      Traditional matchmaking systems (such as Elo) update after every attempt, which penalizes trying risky lines or learning a new track. A time-attack ranking must evaluate your best submitted personal records, not exploratory runs.
      💡 How Our Architecture Solves Both Problems
      The system uses hard portfolio caps on how many tracks count toward your rating (6 tracks for peak pace, 15 for versatility). A specialist who masters a focused selection of tracks can legitimately contend for the World #1 spot against someone who drives all 78 tracks.

      2. Track Difficulty Multipliers (Strength of Field)

      Finishing 1st place on Summer 1 (where nearly 4.5 million players compete) is significantly more competitive than finishing 1st place on a community track with 7,000 players.

      🎯 How Track Weights Work Under the Hood
      Every track in the game is assigned a difficulty multiplier based on its total verified player count:
      • Baseline Anchor (100,000 players): 1.000× baseline multiplier.
      • High-Traffic Campaign Tracks (Summer 1, 4.5M players): 1.297× multiplier (+30% bonus points).
      • Major Campaign Tracks (Desert 1, 1.2M players): 1.193× multiplier.
      • Active Community Tracks (Sky Bound, 142k players): 1.027× multiplier.
      • Smaller Community Tracks (Flying Dreams, 7.3k players): 0.796× multiplier.
      📐 Why We Use a Logarithmic Curve
      Doubling players from 100k to 200k increases competitive density similarly to going from 2M to 4M. A logarithmic curve rewards high-traffic courses without making smaller tracks negligible.
      🛡️ Safety Floors and Ceilings (Clamping)
      Track weights are clamped between 0.65× and 1.85×. Smaller tracks retain a sensible scoring baseline, while mega-tracks cannot inflate ratings beyond reasonable limits.

      3. Smooth Leaderboard Points & World Record Bonus

      Points scale continuously with rank rather than using discrete cutoff tiers.

      4. The Diminishing Weight Ladder (Why We Don't Use a Flat Average)

      When evaluating your top 6 performances for peak pace, the system does not use a simple flat average. If you hold 2 World Records and 4 solid top-5 runs, a flat average would dilute your greatest placements.

      🪜 The 15% Diminishing Weight Ladder (Peak Pace Engine)
      The system sorts your scores from highest to lowest and applies a 15% discount ladder ($\gamma = 0.85$) to each subsequent track:
      • Track 1 (Best Run): 100% weight (1.000)
      • Track 2 (2nd Best): 85% weight (0.850)
      • Track 3 (3rd Best): 72.3% weight (0.723)
      • Track 4 (4th Best): 61.4% weight (0.614)
      • Track 5 (5th Best): 52.2% weight (0.522)
      • Track 6 (6th Best): 44.4% weight (0.444)
      The Result: Your top 2 performances account for over 55% of your total peak score. Your strongest tracks carry the most influence, while still requiring a supporting cast of 4 other strong runs.

      5. The "Trust Meter" (How We Prevent 1-Hit Wonders & Flukes)

      Imagine a brand-new driver joins the game, drives 1 single track on a quiet Tuesday, and sets a World Record. Are they immediately the #1 ranked driver in the world? Of course not - it could be beginner's luck, low competition on that course, or a fluke.

      🛡️ How the "Trust Meter" (Bayesian Shrinkage) Works in Practice
      The system acts like a cautious, experienced judge:
      • The 2 "Ghost Scores": The system starts every player with 2 provisional "ghost scores" of 40 points (representing an average baseline competitor).
      • If you have only 1 track (e.g. 120 points): The system averages your 1 track with the 2 ghost scores: $(120 + 40 + 40) / 3 = \mathbf{66.7\text{ pts}}$. You get credit, but you aren't crowned world champion on day one.
      • As you complete more tracks (2, 4, 6 tracks): Your real driving performances progressively drown out the ghost scores. By 6 tracks, your actual race times account for 75% of your rating, and the provisional training wheels come off completely.

      6. Dual Engines: Peak Quality vs Versatility

      To evaluate a driver comprehensively, the system runs two independent scoring engines with separate portfolio caps:

      🚀 Engine 1: Peak Quality Index (PQI: Peak Pace)
      What it measures: Mechanical pace on your strongest courses.

      • Evaluates your Top 6 best tracks.
      • Uses the 15% discount ladder ($\gamma = 0.85$) to emphasize top placements.
      • Uses 2 baseline anchor scores for sample regularization.
      • Volume Cap: Tracks 7 through 78 give zero points.
      🛡️ Engine 2: Versatility & Depth Index (VDI: Consistency & Breadth)
      What it measures: Sustained pace across diverse track layouts.

      • Evaluates your Top 15 best tracks.
      • Uses a 10% discount ladder ($\gamma = 0.90$) across the larger window.
      • Uses 3 baseline anchor scores (30 pts each) to ensure rating stability.
      • Volume Cap: Tracks 16 through 78 give zero points.

      7. Global Skill Index (GSI): The 70/30 Blend & Standardization

      Peak Quality (PQI) and Versatility (VDI) live on different scales. To combine them fairly without one artificially overpowering the other, the system standardizes both scores using statistical standard scores ($Z$-scores):

      📊 Translating Scores into "Standard Jumps Above Average"
      A $Z$-score translates your raw points into a simple question: "How many standard steps above the average active racer are you?"
      • Active Driver Filter: We calculate the community average using only active competitors with at least 5 completed tracks. This prevents tens of thousands of casual one-time players from dragging down the average.
      • The 70 / 30 Master Recipe: $$\text{Final Rating (GSI)} = 1,000 + 200 \times \left(70\% \text{ Peak Speed Jump} + 30\% \text{ Versatility Jump}\right)$$
      • Centered at 1,000: An average active competitive driver receives exactly 1,000 GSI. Each standard leap above average adds +200 points. Elite World Champions reach 3,500 to 4,100+ GSI (over 12–15 standard leaps above the median racer).
      👑 3,500+ GSI (World Championship Tier)
      Elite drivers who dominate World Records across multiple major campaign and community maps simultaneously.
      🏆 2,000–3,499 GSI (Podium Contenders)
      World-class drivers capable of claiming World Records and consistently finishing top 3 globally.
      ⚡ 1,500–1,999 GSI (Specialists & Top 50)
      Highly skilled drivers holding top 5 finishes on competitive courses.
      👥 1,000–1,499 GSI (Active Competitive Median)
      Solid drivers who regularly set verified competitive times across multiple tracks.

      PolyTrack maps test two fundamentally different driving styles:

      🎯 Technical Discipline (42 Tracks)

      🧭 How Driver Archetypes are Assigned
      The system calculates your score in each discipline (using your Top 6 tracks in that discipline) and compares them:
      • Fullspeed Specialist: 70%+ of your rating comes from Fullspeed tracks (with at least 4 Fullspeed tracks driven).
      • Technical Specialist: 70%+ of your rating comes from Technical tracks (with at least 4 Technical tracks driven).
      • Generalist: Balanced between 30% and 70% with at least 4 tracks driven in both disciplines.
      • Emerging Player: Drivers who have completed fewer than 4 tracks in their primary discipline.

      Design Philosophy and Theoretical Foundations

      Why traditional leaderboard and matchmaking models break down in time-attack racing, and how our architecture solves them.

      Measuring player skill in a deterministic time-attack racing environment presents unique mathematical challenges compared to head-to-head match-based games. In PolyTrack, each competitor $i$ registers a personal best lap time $T_{i,k}$ on track $k \in \{1, \dots, K_{\text{total}}\}$ ($K_{\text{total}} = 78$). The global leaderboard on each track represents a continuous order-statistic distribution:

      Time-Attack Order Statistics
      $$T_{(1),k} < T_{(2),k} < T_{(3),k} < \dots < T_{(N_k),k}$$
      where $N_k$ is the verified participant population on track $k$, and $T_{(1),k}$ represents the global World Record.

      The ecosystem contains two structurally distinct track categories:

      • Official Campaign Tracks (17 Tracks): Ranging from ~370,000 verified drivers on Desert 2 up to nearly 4.5 million on Summer 1 (with 5 tracks exceeding 1 million competitors). These tracks have extensive community participation, where leaderboard margins are separated by hundredths of a second.
      • Community Tracks (61 Tracks): A curated pool of verified player-created circuits. Participation ranges from over 140,000 players on popular courses like Sky Bound down to ~7,350 players on tracks like Flying Dreams.

      Failure Modes of Standard Ranking Systems in Time Attack

      ❌ Linear Summation (Volume Grinding)
      Naïve total point accumulation rewards playtime over driving quality. A player placing 25th on 70 smaller tracks would overtake a World Champion holding 8 World Records on the hardest official campaign tracks.
      ❌ Elo / Matchmaking Models (Exploration Penalty)
      Pairwise Elo models penalize exploratory test runs. Time attack ratings should evaluate verified personal bests rather than intermediate practice attempts.
      ❌ Uniform Track Weighting (Flat Equivalence)
      Treating a track with 7,000 players identically to Summer 1 (4.48 million players) ignores the larger competitive field on high-population tracks.

      Dynamic Strength of Field (SoF) Calibration

      Logarithmic difficulty scaling anchored to verified global player participation.

      Every track $k$ is assigned an intrinsic dynamic weight multiplier ($\omega_k$) calibrated from its verified participant population ($N_k \ge 1$). The baseline weight of $1.000\times$ is anchored at a reference population of $N_0 = 100,000$, governed by a logarithmic scaling function with boundary clamping:

      Dynamic Track Weight Multiplier Formula
      $$\omega_k = \text{clamp}\left(1.0 + \alpha \cdot \log_{10}\left(\frac{N_k}{N_0}\right), \quad \omega_{\min}, \quad \omega_{\max}\right)$$
      where $\alpha = 0.18$, $N_0 = 100,000$, $\omega_{\min} = 0.650$, $\omega_{\max} = 1.850$, and the clamping operator is formally defined as: $$\text{clamp}(x, a, b) \equiv \max(a, \min(b, x))$$

      Mathematical Properties of Logarithmic Difficulty Scaling

      • Extreme Value Distribution Alignment: In competitive time trials, the gap between subsequent percentiles compresses exponentially as population increases. Doubling participation from 100,000 to 200,000 introduces the same relative competitive density increment as scaling from 2,000,000 to 4,000,000. The base-10 logarithm ensures uniform elasticity without allowing flagship tracks to run away with runaway point inflation.
      • Track-Intrinsic Invariance: $\omega_k$ is an environmental property of track $k$, applied identically to all competitors on that course. It remains constant during each rating computation cycle.
      Mega Flagship (Summer 1)
      $N = 4,485,150 \rightarrow \omega = 1.0 + 0.18 \cdot \log_{10}(44.85) = \mathbf{1.297\times}$ dynamic weight.
      Major Campaign (Desert 1)

      Smooth Rank Scoring and World Record Crown

      Continuous logarithmic rank decay coupled with a 1.25x World Record bonus multiplier.

      Instead of discrete tier cliffs (e.g. Formula 1 step drops where 10th receives points but 11th receives zero), points decay smoothly across the entire leaderboard according to a continuous logarithmic rank kernel $R(\text{Rank}_{i,k})$. Holding the global #1 World Record earns a dedicated 1.25× World Record Crown multiplier:

      Individual Track Score Formulation
      $$R(\text{Rank}_{i,k}) = \frac{1}{1 + \beta \cdot \ln(\text{Rank}_{i,k})}, \quad \text{with } \beta = 0.60$$
      $$S_{i,k} = \omega_k \cdot 100.0 \cdot R(\text{Rank}_{i,k}) \cdot \left[1 + 0.25 \cdot \mathbb{I}(\text{Rank}_{i,k} = 1)\right]$$
      where $\text{Rank}_{i,k} \in \mathbb{N}^+ = \{1, 2, 3, \dots\}$ represents the dense re-indexed finishing position of player $i$ on track $k$, and $\mathbb{I}(\text{Rank}_{i,k} = 1)$ is the indicator function evaluating to $1$ exclusively for the #1 World Record holder (and $0$ otherwise).

      Theoretical Rationale of the Logarithmic Kernel

      As lap times approach the limit of a track, each subsequent improvement in position requires significantly greater optimization. The logarithmic rank kernel reflects this by concentrating point differentials at top positions while ensuring that every verified rank provides non-zero, monotonic scoring value.

      Engine 1: Peak Quality Index (PQI Mechanical Ceiling)

      Evaluating absolute peak driving skill across a player's Top 6 performances.

      Peak Quality Index (PQI) evaluates each player's absolute mechanical ceiling. Let $m$ be the total number of distinct tracks driven by player $i$, and let $S_{(1)} \ge S_{(2)} \ge \dots \ge S_{(m)}$ denote their individual track scores sorted in descending order.

      The calculation defines the effective qualifying sample size as $N_{\text{eff}} = \min(6, m)$, evaluates the top scores using a geometric rank discount ($\gamma = 0.85$), and applies Bayesian confidence shrinkage against an uninformative prior:

      Raw PQI Geometric Decay & Bayesian Shrinkage
      $$N_{\text{eff}} = \min(6, m)$$
      $$\text{Raw PQI}_i = \frac{\sum_{r=1}^{N_{\text{eff}}} (0.85)^{r-1} \cdot S_{(r)}}{\sum_{r=1}^{N_{\text{eff}}} (0.85)^{r-1}}$$
      $$\text{PQI}_i = \frac{N_{\text{eff}} \cdot \text{Raw PQI}_i + C_{\text{PQI}} \cdot \mu_{0,\text{PQI}}}{N_{\text{eff}} + C_{\text{PQI}}}, \quad \text{where } C_{\text{PQI}} = 2.0, \; \mu_{0,\text{PQI}} = 40.0$$
      Geometric Weight Sequence: For $N_{\text{eff}} = 6$, the unnormalized weights are $[1.000, 0.850, 0.723, 0.614, 0.522, 0.444]$, concentrating $55\%$ of total weight on the player's top 2 performances.

      Statistical Derivation of Bayesian Shrinkage

      Under a conjugate Gaussian prior $\theta_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_0, \sigma_0^2)$ with sample observations $\bar{X} \sim \mathcal{N}(\theta_i, \sigma^2 / N_{\text{eff}})$, the maximum a posteriori (MAP) posterior expectation is:

      $$\mathbb{E}[\theta_i \mid \mathbf{S}_i] = \frac{N_{\text{eff}} \cdot \bar{S}_{\gamma} + C \cdot \mu_0}{N_{\text{eff}} + C}, \quad \text{with prior confidence scale } C = \frac{\sigma^2}{\sigma_0^2} = 2.0$$
      Boundary Guarantees:
      • Sample Size Regularization: When $N_{\text{eff}} \to 0$, $\text{PQI}_i \to 40.0$, preventing single-race fluke runs from topping the global leaderboard.
      • Volume Invariance: Completing tracks 7 through 78 has zero mathematical effect on PQI ($r > 6 \implies \text{weight} = 0$). A specialist who dominates 6 tracks cannot be overtaken by someone driving 70 tracks at mediocre pace.
      • Full Evidence Saturation: At $N_{\text{eff}} = 6$, empirical performance accounts for $\frac{6}{6+2} = 75\%$ of the rating, with $25\%$ prior anchoring.

      Engine 2: Versatility & Depth Index (VDI Consistency)

      Evaluating sustained competitive breadth across a player's Top 15 performances.

      While PQI measures peak ceiling, Versatility & Depth Index (VDI) rewards sustained excellence across a wider spectrum of tracks. The portfolio window expands to $K = 15$ tracks with gentler geometric decay ($\gamma = 0.90$) and a broader Bayesian prior:

      Raw VDI and Bayesian Shrinkage Formulation
      $$N_{\text{eff}} = \min(15, m)$$
      $$\text{Raw VDI}_i = \frac{\sum_{r=1}^{N_{\text{eff}}} (0.90)^{r-1} \cdot S_{(r)}}{\sum_{r=1}^{N_{\text{eff}}} (0.90)^{r-1}}$$
      $$\text{VDI}_i = \frac{N_{\text{eff}} \cdot \text{Raw VDI}_i + C_{\text{VDI}} \cdot \mu_{0,\text{VDI}}}{N_{\text{eff}} + C_{\text{VDI}}}, \quad \text{where } C_{\text{VDI}} = 3.0, \; \mu_{0,\text{VDI}} = 30.0$$
      Controlled Window Principle: Evaluating exactly 15 tracks ensures players who demonstrate versatility across different map styles are rewarded, while strictly preventing volume grinding beyond track 15. At $N_{\text{eff}} = 15$, empirical evidence accounts for $\frac{15}{15+3} = 83.3\%$ of the rating.

      Engine 3: Global Skill Index (GSI Composite Synthesis)

      Synthesizing 70% Peak Mechanical Pace and 30% Breadth into a standardized rating.

      The final ranking metric is the Global Skill Index (GSI). To combine PQI (peak ceiling) and VDI (depth) onto a unified scale, both metrics are standardized into statistical standard scores ($Z$-scores) relative to the active competitor cohort $\mathcal{P}_{\text{active}} = \{i \mid m_i \ge 5\}$:

      Active Cohort Standardization & GSI Affine Synthesis
      $$Z_{\text{PQI},i} = \frac{\text{PQI}_i - \mu_{\text{PQI}}}{\sigma_{\text{PQI}}}, \quad Z_{\text{VDI},i} = \frac{\text{VDI}_i - \mu_{\text{VDI}}}{\sigma_{\text{VDI}}}$$
      $$\text{GSI}_i = 1000.0 + 200.0 \cdot \left(0.70 \cdot Z_{\text{PQI},i} + 0.30 \cdot Z_{\text{VDI},i}\right)$$
      Active Population Calibration: Restricting the baseline parameters $(\mu_{\text{PQI}}, \sigma_{\text{PQI}})$ and $(\mu_{\text{VDI}}, \sigma_{\text{VDI}})$ to drivers with at least 5 completed tracks prevents the long tail of single-run casual accounts from compressing standard deviations and distorting the competitive scale.

      2-Domain Macro-Discipline Engine (Dual-Axis Mastery)

      Evaluating specialized driving skill across two balanced macro-disciplines: Fullspeed and Technical.

      PolyTrack tracks feature distinct mechanical demands that separate into two core macro-disciplines:

      🏎️ Fullspeed Discipline (36 Tracks)
      High velocity throughout with banking, wallrides, calculated entry angles, and looping layouts. Includes Fullspeed (31 tracks) and Fakespeed (5 tracks).
      🎯 Technical Discipline (42 Tracks)
      Tight cornering, drift angle control, brake taps, rapid transitions, and precision slide arcs. Includes Tech (14 tracks), Speedtech (14 tracks), and Speedfun (14 tracks).
      Mathematical Formulation (Discipline Rating)
      For each domain $D \in \{\text{Fullspeed}, \text{Technical}\}$, let $m_D$ be the count of tracks completed by player $i$ in domain $D$. The discipline rating evaluates their Top 6 best runs ($K=6$) with geometric decay ($\gamma = 0.85$) and Bayesian shrinkage ($\mu_0 = 40.0, C = 2.0$):
      $$N_{\text{eff}, D} = \min(6, m_D)$$
      $$\text{Raw Score}(i, D) = \frac{\sum_{r=1}^{N_{\text{eff}, D}} (0.85)^{r-1} \cdot S_{(r), D}}{\sum_{r=1}^{N_{\text{eff}, D}} (0.85)^{r-1}}$$
      $$\text{Rating}(i, D) = \frac{N_{\text{eff}, D} \cdot \text{Raw Score}(i, D) + 2.0 \cdot 40.0}{N_{\text{eff}, D} + 2.0}$$
      *Note: If $m_D = 0$ (no completed tracks in domain $D$), $\text{Rating}(i, D) = 0.0$ (Unranked).*

      Discipline Bias Ratio & Archetype Partitioning

      A player's competitive balance between the two physics disciplines is defined by the Discipline Bias Ratio:

      Discipline Bias Ratio
      $$\text{Bias}(i) = \frac{\text{Rating}(i, \text{Fullspeed})}{\text{Rating}(i, \text{Fullspeed}) + \text{Rating}(i, \text{Technical})}$$
      *Note: Defaults to $0.50$ (neutral balance) if a player has zero total rating across both disciplines.*
      Driver Archetype Classification Decision Rules
      Driver archetypes are formally classified according to the following decision partition:
      $$\text{Archetype}(i) = \begin{cases} \textbf{Fullspeed Specialist} & \text{if } \text{Bias}(i) \ge 0.70 \text{ and } m_{\text{FS}} \ge 4 \\ \textbf{Technical Specialist} & \text{if } \text{Bias}(i) \le 0.30 \text{ and } m_{\text{Tech}} \ge 4 \\ \textbf{Generalist} & \text{if } 0.30 < \text{Bias}(i) < 0.70 \text{ and } m_{\text{FS}} \ge 4 \text{ and } m_{\text{Tech}} \ge 4 \\ \textbf{Player (Emerging)} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
      🏎️ Fullspeed Specialist
      $\text{Bias}(i) \ge 0.70$ and $\ge 4$ Fullspeed tracks driven. Excels at high momentum conservation, loops, and wallrides.
      🎯 Technical Specialist
      $\text{Bias}(i) \le 0.30$ and $\ge 4$ Technical tracks driven. Excels at tight drift management, slide initiation, and brake tapping.
      ⚡ Generalist
      $0.30 < \text{Bias}(i) < 0.70$ and $\ge 4$ tracks driven in both domains. Possesses world-class versatility across both fast and technical courses.
      🏁 Player (Emerging)
      Default archetype for emerging players with $< 4$ completed tracks in their primary discipline.

      Differentiating Campaign vs Community track orientation from driving physics discipline.

      It is important to distinguish between Mechanical Discipline (Fullspeed vs Technical physics, Section 07) and Ecosystem Domain (Official Campaign vs Community tracks). Every player is classified based on the distribution of their points across these two ecosystems:

      Domain Bias Formula & Classification Partition
      $$\text{Domain Bias}(i) = \frac{\sum_{k \in \text{Main}} S_{i,k}}{\sum_{k \in \text{Main}} S_{i,k} + \sum_{k \in \text{Community}} S_{i,k}}$$
      $$\text{Classification}(i) = \begin{cases} \textbf{Main Specialist} & \text{if } \text{Domain Bias}(i) \ge 0.70 \\ \textbf{Community Specialist} & \text{if } \text{Domain Bias}(i) \le 0.30 \\ \textbf{All Rounder} & \text{if } 0.30 < \text{Domain Bias}(i) < 0.70 \end{cases}$$
      🔵 Main Specialist
      Players who earn 70% or more of their points on official campaign tracks.
      🟣 Community Specialist
      Players who earn 70% or more of their points on community tracks.

      Data Sanitization, Alt Consolidation & Dense Re-Indexing

      Rigorous data preprocessing pipeline ensuring legitimate human competition and fair rank spacing.

      Before applying mathematical rating engines, raw leaderboard ingest undergoes a multi-stage sanitization pipeline to eliminate bot distortion and artificial rank dilution:

      1. [TAS] Bot Rejection
      All tool-assisted speedruns and accounts prefixed with [TAS] are purged from leaderboards to preserve pure human competitive integrity.
      2. Canonical Identity Consolidation
      Verified alternate accounts belonging to the same human competitor are mapped to their canonical primary identity. On every individual track, only the player's single fastest personal best time is retained; all duplicate entries are eliminated.
      3. Dense Rank Re-Indexing
      Following alt and bot elimination, leaderboard standings are compacted into contiguous integer ranks $\text{Rank} \in \{1, 2, 3, \dots, N_{\text{human}}\}$. This ensures point formulas $R(\text{Rank})$ operate on true human relative performance without gaps.

      Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

      Common questions regarding scoring mechanics, edge cases, and ratings.
      Q: Does grinding all 78 tracks give someone an unfair advantage?
